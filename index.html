📚 Study Guide

Review the key ideas, then test what you know.

UNIT 2

1. Good Digital Citizenship

  • Be respectful and responsible online.
  • Pause before posting, messaging, or sharing.
  • Disagree with ideas respectfully; do not attack people.
UNIT 2

2. Personal Information

  • Keep your address, passwords, and live location private.
  • Do not share personal details with strangers.
  • Ask a trusted adult when something feels suspicious.
UNIT 2

3. Passwords & Accounts

  • Use strong, unique passwords.
  • Simple patterns like 12345678 are easy to guess.
  • If an account may be at risk, change the password and ask for help.
UNIT 2

4. Downloads, Malware & Pop-Ups

  • A download copies a file/program from the internet to your device.
  • Malware is harmful software.
  • Adware displays unwanted ads. Avoid unknown downloads and pop-ups.
UNIT 2

5. Phishing & Scams

  • Phishing messages try to trick people into sharing information or clicking unsafe links.
  • Watch for strange senders, unexpected links, urgent demands, and attachments.
  • Do not click before verifying the message.
UNIT 2

6. Cyberbullying & Peer Pressure

  • Cyberbullying is hurtful behavior using digital tools.
  • Do not join in, like, or share hurtful posts.
  • Block/report the behavior and tell a trusted adult.
UNIT 2

7. Digital Footprint

  • Your digital footprint is the trail of information you leave online.
  • Posts, comments, photos, and shares can shape how others see you.
  • Think about your audience before posting.
UNIT 2

8. Geotagging

  • Geotagging can attach location information to a photo or post.
  • Location details may reveal where you were.
  • Think carefully before sharing your location publicly.
UNIT 3

9. Types of Communication

  • Texting: quick, casual messages.
  • Email: organized communication for school/professional needs.
  • Video calls let people see and hear one another.
  • Choose a method that fits the situation.
UNIT 3

10. Verbal & Nonverbal

  • Verbal communication uses words, spoken or written.
  • Nonverbal cues include facial expressions, gestures, posture, and eye contact.
  • Tone and body language affect meaning.
UNIT 3

11. Textisms & Digital Language

  • Textisms are shortened forms like LOL, CU L8R, or u.
  • All caps can look like shouting.
  • An ellipsis (…) can sound hesitant or unclear.
UNIT 3

12. Formal vs. Informal

  • Informal language often works with friends.
  • Formal language is usually better for teachers and professional situations.
  • Consider your audience, purpose, spelling, and tone.
UNIT 3

13. Netiquette

  • Netiquette means etiquette and respectful behavior online.
  • Be polite, stay on topic, and avoid insults or all-caps shouting.
  • Remember there is a real person on the other side of the screen.
UNIT 3

14. Professional Emails

  • Use a clear subject and polite greeting.
  • Explain your question clearly; close politely and identify yourself.
  • Proofread and check attachments before sending.
UNIT 3

15. Video Call Etiquette

  • Confirm date, time, and link; test your device.
  • Choose a suitable place and join on time.
  • Listen, avoid interrupting, and prepare questions.
UNIT 3

16. Online Learning

  • Use a schedule or checklist to organize work.
  • Ask your instructor for help when confused.
  • Review directions and use the communication method recommended.
Safety reminders: STOP → LOOK → THINK → CHECK before clicking or sharing. For cyberbullying: STOP → BLOCK → REPORT → TELL a trusted adult.
